Pattern

Content and tone

Saturdaze sounds like the parent who already looked at the forecast: specific, calm and brief. This page shows the rules at work inside real screens; the full rule set and label library live on Content.

Overview

  • Specific over general. "Sunny Saturday for the lavender, a cloudy Sunday for the Rec Room" instead of "Your weekend is ready".
  • Plain words. Lock, swap, regenerate, share. No "itinerary optimisation", no "AI-powered".
  • Calm. No exclamation marks, no "Oops", no "Awesome!". Good news is stated; bad news is stated with the next step.
  • Brief. One idea per sentence. Most sub lines are under ten words: "Ages shape the picks."
  • Written out. The mocks avoid contractions ("did not", "it is", "will not"); keep that so the voice stays even.
  • Sentence case everywhere: titles, buttons, tabs, labels, menu items. Proper nouns keep their capitals (Terre Bleu, Port Credit, Rec Room).

Structure

A decision, line by line

  1. Title: the questionVerb and object, ending in a question mark. The parent can answer it without reading further.
  2. Sub line: the reassurance or the costWhat will not change, or what will be lost. One short sentence.
  3. Body: the specificsNames and times from this weekend, separated by middle dots.
  4. Buttons: Cancel and the verbThe primary repeats the title's verb, with the object when it fits: "Regenerate Saturday".

Responsive behaviour

  • Write for 360px first. A button label that wraps or a title over two lines at 360px is too long; shorten the words, do not shrink the type.
  • Copy does not change by breakpoint, with one exception: day-header buttons drop their visible label under 720px and keep it as aria-label ("Regenerate Saturday").
  • Truncate only names that come from data (place names, emails) with an ellipsis and the full text available; never truncate system copy.
  • Meta lines wrap at a middle dot, never inside a value: keep "1 h 36 min" together with non-breaking spaces.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Plain language is an accessibility feature: short sentences, common words, one idea per line. Headings describe their section (2.4.6); link text makes sense out of context (2.4.4): "Go to this weekend", never "Click here".

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReads each control's name in turn; names must be unique within a view ("Regenerate Saturday", "Regenerate Sunday").

Focus

Not applicable to copy itself; focus rules are on Forms and Dialogs and overlays.

Labelling

  • Icon-only buttons name the verb and the object: "Swap Lavender fields", "Lock Saturday".
  • Dates and times sit in <time datetime>. Symbols that read badly get words for screen readers: "22° / 14°" is announced as "high 22, low 14" through a visually hidden label where it matters.
  • Abbreviations stay common ("min", "h", "Sat"); no initialisms a parent would have to guess.
  • Language is set on <html lang="en">; French place names keep their spelling (Terre Bleu).

Motion

Touch

Error and confirmation formulas

  • Error: [what did not happen or is wrong] + [how to fix it]. "That file is not a JPG, PNG or WebP image." "Enter an age from 0 to 120." No "Error:", no codes in the message, no "you failed to".
  • Confirmation: title "[Verb] [object]?", sub line "[consequence]", buttons Cancel and "[Verb]". "Reject this suggestion?" · "The reason is shown to the person who sent it." · Reject.
  • Full library of labels and messages: Content.
