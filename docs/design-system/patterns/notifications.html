Pattern

Notifications

Saturdaze talks to a family in four ways: a toast for what they just did, a banner for a condition that holds, a well for something worth knowing, and a status row for work in progress. Pick by how long the message stays true.

Saturdaze has no notification centre or inbox. The Friday preview is an email; nothing in the app needs to be caught up on later.

When to use

  • Toast when the person did something and the result is easy to miss or off-screen: "Removed Costco run from Sunday", "Link copied", "Saved Mae". Not for something the control already shows.
  • Banner when the page or form cannot do its job, or will do it differently, until something changes: "That email and password did not match", "You are offline. Changes will save when you are back."
  • Well to explain the content next to it: why Swim lessons cannot be swapped, the short version of the privacy policy. Not an event, so never announced.
  • Status row while the system works on this page for more than a couple of seconds: weekend generating, "Approved · Bronte Creek Maple Magic" after a review action.
  • Dialog only when the message needs a decision or has details to read; see Dialogs and overlays.

Anatomy of a toast

  1. Icon by tone: check circle for success, alert for warnings and errors, info otherwise.
  2. Message: one line, the object and the outcome.
  3. Action (optional, at most one): "Undo", "Try again", "View".
  4. Dismiss: an icon button labelled "Dismiss", always present.

Auto-dismiss rules

  • No toast leaves in under 8 seconds (2.2.1). One with an action stays at least 10 seconds, and Undo is never the only way to recover something precious.
  • The timer pauses while the pointer is over the toast region or focus is inside it, and restarts in full when it leaves.
  • Errors never dismiss themselves. Banners never dismiss themselves; a caution or info banner may have a close button.
  • Toasts survive a route change within the app, so "Saved Mae" still shows after the dialog closes and Family re-renders.

Grouping

  • Newest at the bottom of the region, nearest the thumb; older ones move up.
  • At most three visible (one under 720px). A fourth replaces the oldest non-error toast.
  • Repeats merge: three errands removed in a row become "Removed 3 errands · Undo", which restores all three.
  • A new error replaces an older error about the same thing instead of stacking.

Responsive behaviour

  • Under 720px the toast region spans the content width and sits above the bottom nav, clearing it the same way the nav clears the browser chrome (ADR-005). One toast at a time.
  • From 720px the region is 24rem wide in the bottom-right corner, 24px from the edges, with up to three toasts.
  • Banners span the content column; .banner--page runs edge to edge under the top bar for whole-app conditions such as being offline.
  • Toasts never cover a focused element or the primary action: if a phone dialog sheet is open, the toast waits until it closes.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The toast region is in the DOM from page load, empty, so that messages written into it are announced (4.1.3). Success and info use role="status"; errors use role="alert". A banner that appears in response to an action (the sign-in error) uses role="alert"; a banner present on load has no live role. Wells are plain content. See the APG Alert pattern.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabToasts sit at the end of the DOM; Tab reaches their action and Dismiss after the page content. Focus is never moved into a toast.
Enter / SpaceRuns the toast's action or dismisses it.
EscWith focus in a toast, dismisses it and returns focus to where it was.

Focus

Notifications never take focus, with one exception: after a failed submit, a form's error banner or summary takes it (see Forms). Undo restores focus to the restored item.

Labelling

The message text is the whole announcement, so it must stand alone: "Removed Costco run from Sunday", not "Removed". Icons are decorative; the dismiss button is "Dismiss".

Motion

Toasts rise 4px and fade in over --durationNormal and fade out the same way; under reduced motion they appear and disappear at once.

Touch

Toasts are at least 52px tall; Dismiss is 32px and the action at least 24px, 8px apart. No swipe-to-dismiss, so nothing depends on a gesture.

Content

  • Toasts: past tense, object first or verb first, no "successfully", no exclamation marks: "Saved Mae", "Link copied".
  • Banners: what is true now, then what it means: "Sunday's forecast is not in yet. The plan assumes a dry day."
  • Errors: what did not happen, and the next step: "Could not copy the link." + "Try again".
  • Wells: a short bold title and one or two sentences of explanation.
