Foundation

Elevation

Elevation separates the page from what sits on it for a moment. Surfaces step up in colour, overlays cast a shadow, and nine named layers decide what covers what.

Tokens: --shadow*, --zIndex*, shadow coloursSurface first, shadow second

Overview

Elevation tells a family what is part of the page and what is on top of it for a moment. Saturdaze keeps it flat: most surfaces are separated by colour and a hairline, and real shadows are saved for things that float.

  • Four shadow levels (2, 4, 16, 28) and a focus ring, with themed shadow colours.
  • Three surfaces and a recess: canvas, surface, raised, and wells inside surfaces.
  • Nine z-order layers from page content to tooltips.

Shadow levels

Four shadow levels, named by Fluent's blur depth, plus a focus ring. A higher number means the surface is further from the page and more temporary: a hover lifts to 16, an overlay that will close sits at 28.

Each shadow mixes a tight ambient layer (contact) with a softer key layer (distance). Their colours are theme tokens: --colorNeutralShadowAmbient, --colorNeutralShadowKey and --colorNeutralShadowKeyDarker, ink at 4, 6 and 12% in light and black at 30, 40 and 55% in dark.

Surface hierarchy

Depth in Saturdaze comes from surface colour first and shadow second. There are three levels and one recess:

  1. Canvas (--colorNeutralBackground2): the page itself, cream in light.
  2. Surface (--colorNeutralBackground1): cards, lists, inputs, panels. White in light, with a decorative --colorNeutralStroke2 edge and no shadow at rest.
  3. Raised (--colorNeutralBackgroundRaised + --shadow28): dialogs, menus, the bottom nav, toasts. Anything that floats above the page and will go away.

Wells (--colorNeutralBackground3) sit inside a surface and read as recessed: locked rows, chips, segment tracks, copy fields. They never take a shadow.

Elevation in the dark theme

Shadows barely read on a dark page, so the dark theme carries elevation with lightness instead: the higher a surface, the lighter it is.

LevelLightDark
CanvasCream 50Umber 900 (darkest)
SurfaceWhiteUmber 850
RaisedWhite + shadowUmber 800 + a darker shadow
Well, hoverCream 100 (darker than the surface)Umber 750 (lighter than the surface)
  • Shadows get darker, not lighter. The shadow colours switch from ink at 4 to 12% to black at 30 to 55%, so the edge of a menu or dialog still separates from the umber page.
  • Edges take over. The decorative strokes become cream at 10 and 18%, so a card's border does the work its shadow cannot.
  • Wells flip direction. In light a well is darker than its card; in dark it is lighter. Both read as "a different layer" without a shadow.
  • The scrim deepens from ink at 40% to black at 60%, so a dialog still stands out from the page behind it.

Z-order

Nine named layers, spaced so a new layer can slot in without renumbering. A component never sets a raw z-index; it picks its layer here.

LayerTokenValueWhat lives there
Base--zIndexBasePage content
Raised--zIndexRaisedA timeline disc above its rail; a stacked avatar above its neighbour
Sticky--zIndexStickyThe day header that sticks while the day scrolls
Top bar--zIndexTopBarTop bar, site bar, admin navigation bar
Bottom nav--zIndexBottomNavThe floating bottom nav, above content and any sticky header
Dialog--zIndexDialogDialog panels and their scrim, above all navigation
Popover--zIndexPopoverMenus and select popovers; above dialogs because a menu can open from inside one
Toast--zIndexToastToasts, above dialogs so "Saved" is seen after a dialog closes over it
Tooltip--zIndexTooltipTooltips and the skip link: the top of everything

Layers only compete inside the same stacking context. Dialogs, menus, toasts and tooltips render at the end of body (Angular CDK Overlay in the product), so their tokens decide the order; a transform or filter on an ancestor would trap them, which is why overlays are never rendered inside a card.

Usage

  • Surface before shadow. Resting cards and lists are separated by colour and a decorative edge, not by a shadow. Shadow is for things that move or float.
  • One level per surface. Pick the level for what the surface is (resting control 4, lifted card 16, overlay 28); never stack two shadows or invent an in-between.
  • Hover lifts one step. An interactive card goes from no shadow to --shadow16 and rises 2px; a primary button from 4 to 16. Only under (hover: hover), never on touch.
  • Wells never cast. A recessed surface takes a fill, not a shadow.
  • Layers by token. Use --zIndex*; if two layers need a new order, change the scale, not one component.
  • Overlays at the root. Anything at dialog level or above is portalled out of the page flow so its layer is honoured.

Accessibility

  • Shadow is never the only edge. Every raised surface also has a --colorNeutralStroke2 border (menus, toasts, the dialog specimen) or a scrim (dialogs), because shadows can be faint on some screens and vanish entirely in forced colours.
  • Forced colours. Windows high contrast drops box shadows and replaces fills; borders become CanvasText, so surfaces stay separated by their edges.
  • Focus is an outline. The focus ring is a 2px outline in --colorStrokeFocus2 that survives forced colours; --shadowFocusRing is used only where an outline would be clipped, and must then be paired with a transparent outline so forced colours still show focus.
  • Focus not obscured (2.4.11). Sticky and floating layers (day header, top bar, bottom nav) must not hide the focused element; see layout.
  • Modal layers trap focus and inert the page below (dialogs); popovers and toasts do not. See dialog.
