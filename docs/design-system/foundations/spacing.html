Spacing

Spacing groups what belongs together and separates what does not. One 4px scale, a few rules about inside and between, and a table of padding by component size.

Tokens: --spacing*, --controlHeight*4px grid · rem

Overview

Saturdaze spacing is a 4px grid with thirteen named steps from 0 to 56px, plus three control heights. Most screens use only four of them: 8px between inline items, 12px for compact padding, 16px for cards and gaps, 24px for dialogs and groups.

  • Two sets, one scale: --spacingHorizontal* and --spacingVertical* share values and say which axis a space is on.
  • Three nudges (2, 6, 10px) for tight spots inside small components.
  • Control heights of 32, 40 and 48px for buttons, filter chips and segments.

The 4px scale

Every space is a multiple of 4px, written in rem so it grows with the reader's font size. Fluent's naming is kept: the size steps run XXS to XXXXXL, and each exists twice, as --spacingHorizontal* and --spacingVertical*, with the same values. Use the horizontal token for inline space (left, right, column gaps) and the vertical one for block space (top, bottom, row gaps) so the intent survives a later retune.

Nudges

Three steps sit off the 4px grid: 2px (XXS), 6px (SNudge) and 10px (MNudge). They come from Fluent and exist for tight, small parts where 4 or 8px is visibly too little or too much. They are allowed inside small components only:

  • 2px: between stacked menu items and nav links, between rating stars, the gap above a chip icon.
  • 6px: a field label to its input, a chip's icon to its label, menu panel padding, the inset of a photo label.
  • 10px: chip side padding, the gap inside a card (title, meta, chips), a menu item's icon gap and vertical padding, a well's icon gap.

Never use a nudge between components, for page or section spacing, or to fix alignment: if something is 2px out, the component is wrong, not the spacing.

Inside and between

Space shows what belongs together. Space inside a component is always smaller than the space around it, so a card's title, meta and chips read as one thing and the next card reads as another.

  • Inside a component: 24px or less (XXL), usually 10 to 16px.
  • Between components in a stack: 12 to 16px for related items (cards in a list, fields in a form), 24px between groups.
  • Between page sections: --layoutSpaceSection, 32px on phones and 40px from 720px. Page bottom and landing bands use --layoutSpacePage (64px). Both are on layout.
  • Padding, not margins. Containers own their padding and stacks own their gaps (.stack, .cluster, grid gap). Components do not set outer margins, so they can be placed anywhere.

Control heights

Three control heights keep buttons, filter chips, segments and inputs on a shared rhythm. Medium is the default; fields and phone page actions use the 44px comfortable target instead (--targetComfortable, on accessibility).

Usage

  • Only tokens. No raw px or rem for padding, margin or gap in component CSS. If a value is missing, the design is off-grid, not the scale.
  • Smaller inside than between. A component's inner spacing is always less than the gap that separates it from its neighbours.
  • Nudges stay small. 2, 6 and 10px only inside small components (chips, menus, fields, cards), never between them.
  • Gaps over margins. Lay out siblings with gap on the parent (.stack, .cluster, grids); a component never pushes its neighbours with an outer margin.
  • Horizontal for inline, vertical for block. padding: var(--spacingVerticalM) var(--spacingHorizontalL) reads as "12 top and bottom, 16 left and right".
  • Optical exceptions are written down. A dialog close button pulls itself 4px into the corner (calc(-1 * var(--spacingVerticalXS))); a locked or selected card trims 1px of padding to absorb its 2px border. Keep such corrections in the component and comment them.

Accessibility

  • Target spacing (2.5.8). Small 32px buttons and 24px chips keep at least 4px (XS) between neighbours and never sit in a tight cluster of more than one row, so each target keeps its own 24px circle.
  • Text spacing (1.4.12). Text containers (cards, wells, banners, dialogs, rows) size by padding rather than fixed heights, so a reader who increases line, letter or word spacing gets taller boxes, not clipped text.
  • Zoom. Spacing is in rem; at 200% text size every gap doubles with the text, so proportions hold.
  • Proximity is structure for sighted readers only. Groups that space alone implies (a fieldset of related fields, the parts of a card) also get the matching markup: fieldset and legend, a heading, a list.
