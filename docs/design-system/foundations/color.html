Foundation

Color

Colour tells a family what they can do, where they are and how things went. Cream and white carry the content, coral marks the one action that matters, and every pairing is checked for contrast in both themes.

WCAG 2.2 AA · 98 pairsTokens: --globalColor*, --color*Light ships · dark proposed

Overview

Saturdaze colour is warm and quiet: a cream canvas, white cards, slate ink, and one coral for the thing that matters on the screen. Status colours say how something went; four soft palette hues (sun, sky, leaf, indoor) sort activities, weather and the people in the family.

  • Two tiers. Primitive ramps (--globalColor*) hold raw values. Alias roles (--color*, Fluent UI v9 names per ADR-013) give them a job and are the only colour tokens components read.
  • Themes swap roles. Light lives on :root; dark re-points the same roles at different primitives. No component knows which theme it is in.
  • Accessible by construction. All 98 foreground and background pairs the components use pass WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes; the mock values that did not were changed and logged (D02 to D10).

Primitive ramps

Primitives are raw values, named by hue and step: 50 is the lightest, 950 the darkest. A ramp only has the steps a role needs, so the gaps are deliberate. Nothing outside tokens.css reads a --globalColor* token; components and pages read the roles below.

Coral

Brand. 400 is the mock coral, now decorative only (brand mark); 500 is the accessible fill; 600 is brand text and the focus ring; 300 is the dark-theme fill with a 950 label.

Slate

Light-theme ink: 900 body text, 600 secondary, 500 hints, 400 accessible strokes, 300 disabled text.

Cream

Light-theme canvas (50), wells and hover (100), pressed (200).

Umber

Dark-theme neutrals: canvas 900, surface 850, raised 800, wells 750; text 50, 200 and 300.

Forest

Success and locked. 500 is the solid fill the mock used; 600 is text on the tint (D07).

Leaf

Outdoor palette and map land. 300 is the mock leaf chip.

Sun

Warm weather and warning. 300 is the mock sun; 600 the filled star (D09); 700 warning text.

Sky

Info, cool weather, travel and map water. 200 is the mock sky chip; 700 info text.

Lavender

Indoor palette. 200 is the mock indoor chip; 700 text on the tint.

Terracotta

Danger. 500 is the brick-red fill (D08); 700 the mock warn-deep text; 300 and 400 for the dark theme.

White and black

White is the light-theme card and the label on solid fills. Black only appears inside the dark-theme scrim and shadows, as an alpha.

Semantic roles

Roles are what components read. Each name says what the colour is for (Foreground for text and icons, Background for fills, Stroke for borders), so the same token works in both themes. Values below follow the theme of this page; switch with the toggle in the navigation or press t.

  • Surfaces lighten as they rise. Light puts white cards on a cream canvas; dark puts umber 850 cards on an umber 900 canvas, and raised surfaces (dialogs, menus) on umber 800.
  • Brand lightens, its label darkens. The fill moves from coral 500 to coral 300 and the label flips from white to coral 950, so the pair stays above 7:1 instead of glowing.
  • Status and palette tints become deep tints (Sky 900, Forest 900, Sun 900, Terracotta 900, Lavender 900) with light text from the 200 and 300 steps.
  • Decorative strokes become cream alphas (10% and 18%) so card edges still separate; shadows darken (see elevation).
  • Photos do not change. Text over a photo is white on a 72% overlay in both themes (--colorNeutralForegroundStaticInverted on --colorBackgroundOverlayStrong).

How the theme is chosen (data-theme, prefers-color-scheme, color-scheme) is on theming.

Adding a brand theme

A brand theme (a partner build, a seasonal skin) is one more [data-theme] block in tokens.css that overrides alias tokens only. Components, component tokens and neutrals stay as they are.

  1. Add the hue as a primitive ramp in the global tier if no existing ramp fits. A brand needs at least a tint (100), a hover tint (200), a fill (500 to 700) and a darker hover and pressed step.
  2. Override the twelve brand roles and the focus ring (--colorStrokeFocus2). Leave status colours alone: danger must still read differently from the new brand (D08), and the brand must not borrow a status or palette hue.
  3. Write the dark variant from the roles: a lighter fill with a dark label, a deep tint for --colorBrandBackground2.
  4. Add the brand pairs to tokens/contrast-pairs.json for the new theme and run check_contrast.py; every pair in the table below must still pass.
  5. Re-export tokens.json and check the Weekend, Family and dialog mocks in both themes.
/* Example: a harbour-blue skin. It borrows Sky to stay inside existing
   primitives; a real theme adds its own ramp so it does not look like info. */
[data-theme="harbour"] {
  --colorBrandBackground: var(--globalColorSky700);
  --colorBrandBackgroundHover: var(--globalColorSky800);
  --colorBrandBackgroundPressed: var(--globalColorSky900);
  --colorBrandBackground2: var(--globalColorSky100);
  --colorBrandBackground2Hover: var(--globalColorSky200);
  --colorBrandBackgroundStatic: var(--globalColorSky300);
  --colorBrandForeground1: var(--globalColorSky700);
  --colorBrandForeground2: var(--globalColorSky800);
  --colorBrandForegroundLink: var(--globalColorSky700);
  --colorBrandForegroundLinkHover: var(--globalColorSky800);
  --colorBrandStroke1: var(--globalColorSky700);
  --colorBrandStroke2: var(--globalColorSky100);
  --colorStrokeFocus2: var(--globalColorSky800);
}

Usage

  • Read colour by role, never by primitive or hex. Text uses a Foreground token, fills a Background token, borders a Stroke token. A --globalColor* or a hex value in component CSS is a bug.
  • One coral fill per view. The primary button is the only solid coral; the tint (--colorBrandBackground2) marks where you are. --colorBrandBackgroundStatic, the mock coral, is for the brand mark and never sits behind text (D02).
  • Three text strengths, in order. Foreground1 for content, Foreground2 for meta and labels, Foreground3 for hints and placeholders. Nothing fainter than Foreground3 carries text (D04); ForegroundDisabled is only for disabled controls.
  • Decorative or accessible stroke. Card edges and dividers use Stroke1/Stroke2; anything a person must see to use a control (input border, quiet button, switch track, dashed add row) uses StrokeAccessible (D06, D28).
  • Status means status, palette means category. Sun, sky, leaf and indoor tag activities, weather and people; they never say "succeeded" or "failed". Locked uses success forest because it is a confirmed state.
  • Never colour alone. Links are underlined (D20), pressed tinted chips gain an edge (D26), the rating star changes shape as well as colour (D09), status banners carry an icon and a title.
  • Over photos use --colorNeutralForegroundStaticInverted on --colorBackgroundOverlayStrong at 12px or larger (D11).
  • A new pairing is a new contract. If a component puts a foreground on a background that is not in the table below, add it to contrast-pairs.json and run the check before shipping.

Accessibility and contrast

Every foreground and background that a component puts together is listed in tokens/contrast-pairs.json (98 pairs) and checked in both themes. The ratios below are computed live in your browser from the tokens. Text needs 4.5:1 (1.4.3); edges, focus indicators and meaningful graphics need 3:1 (1.4.11, 2.4.7). Rows marked 3:1 show an icon in the foreground colour instead of text.

One pair is listed at 1:1 on purpose: the white switch thumb on the off track. The thumb is decorative; the track edge (StrokeAccessible, 3:1) and the thumb's position carry the state.

Contrast preferences

  • prefers-contrast: more turns both decorative strokes into StrokeAccessible and moves Foreground2 and Foreground3 one step darker, so every edge and every line of meta text gets stronger.
  • forced-colors: active (Windows high contrast) hands the focus ring to Highlight and every stroke to CanvasText; the system palette replaces fills, so no state may depend on a fill alone.
