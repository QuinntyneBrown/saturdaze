Foundation

Typography

Type in Saturdaze is plain and readable: one family, a short scale and thirteen named roles, so a card title or a dialog title looks the same wherever it appears.

Overview

Saturdaze sets everything in Inter, falling back to the system UI font, with a monospace for codes and times. The scale is small and practical: 15px body for dense planner rows, 17px card titles, 20 to 26px for section and page titles, and a 44px display size the landing page uses once.

  • Thirteen roles (--typography*) bundle weight, size, line height and family into one font shorthand. Components read roles.
  • Rem throughout, so type follows the reader's font size and zoom.
  • Responsive at the top only: the four largest sizes step down under 380px and up from 1024px through token retunes in tokens.css.

Families

One sans for everything a family reads, one monospace for strings they might copy or compare: invite codes, error references, the admin activity log's times.

Type roles

Components set type with one role shorthand (font: var(--typographyTitle2)), never with a loose size and weight. Thirteen roles cover every text style in the mocks; a few components adjust one property on top of a role (the hero title tightens its line height, menu items drop Body1 to 14px), and that adjustment is written in components.css, not improvised in a page.

Measure

Running text stops at 60 characters (--layoutMeasure, owned by layout), roughly ten to twelve words a line. Legal prose uses --layoutProseMaxWidth (680px) with the relaxed line height. Cards, rows and dialogs are narrower than the measure already, so the limit matters on wide pages: the landing hero lede (540px), empty-state bodies, the docs you are reading.

Saturdaze drafts Saturday and Sunday around swim lessons, church and the weather, then fills the rest with things your family will say yes to. Lock what you love, swap what you do not, and the rest of the day re-flows around it.

Responsive type

Only the four largest sizes change with the window, and they change in tokens.css, not in components. Two media queries retune the tokens: @media (max-width: 23.6875rem) (under 380px) steps the hero sizes down so "This weekend" and the landing title fit a small phone without wrapping awkwardly, and @media (min-width: 64rem) (1024px and up) steps them up for desktop. Components keep writing font: var(--typographyTitle1) and pick up the new size; nothing below 20px moves, so body text never shrinks on a phone.

The breakpoints are in rem (D24), so a reader who sets a larger default font reaches the smaller layout and the smaller hero sizes sooner, which is the point: the text they asked for still fits.

Why body is 15px

Body text is 15px (--fontSizeBase400, 0.9375rem), one step below the 16px most systems default to. This is a product decision carried over from the mocks (--sd-fs-base: 15px), not an oversight:

  • Inter runs large. Its tall x-height makes 15px read like 16px in most other faces, and the planner's dense rows (time, title, place, chips) fit a 360px phone without wrapping every title.
  • It is rem, not px. 0.9375rem follows the reader's browser setting: someone who sets 20px as their default gets 18.75px body text, and zoom scales everything (1.4.4).
  • Nothing below 12px. The 10 and 11px labels in the mocks were raised to 12px (D12), so the bottom of the scale is still comfortable.
  • Line height 1.5 gives body text the spacing 1.4.12 expects without any extra rule.

One trade-off to watch: iOS Safari zooms the page when a field whose text is under 16px gets focus. Fields use Body1 (15px), so if that zoom becomes a complaint in testing, fields are the first place to move to 16px.

Usage

  • Use a role, not a size. font: var(--typographyBody2), never font-size: 13px. If no role fits, the design needs a decision, not a new number.
  • One title per level. Page title (Title1), section and dialog title (Title2), card title (Title3), row title (Subtitle1). Do not skip a level to get a different size; change the heading level and the role together.
  • Sentence case everywhere, including buttons, titles and tabs. Uppercase only for overlines and small tags, always with letter spacing, and never more than four words.
  • Weights mean something. 400 reads, 500 names, 600 acts or titles, 700 heads a page or section. Do not bold words inside a sentence for emphasis; restructure the sentence.
  • Numbers line up. Times, counts and figures use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums where they stack (timeline clocks, stat figures, count chips).
  • Long text has a measure. Cap paragraphs at --layoutMeasure; balance headings with text-wrap: balance.
  • Truncate only what can be recovered. Place names in a list may truncate with the full name in the detail view; titles, labels and errors wrap.

Accessibility

  • Resize text (1.4.4). Every size, breakpoint and line length is in rem or ch, so 200% browser text size or zoom enlarges text and moves the layout to its phone form instead of clipping.
  • Text spacing (1.4.12). Body uses line height 1.5; text containers size with padding rather than fixed heights, so reader overrides of line, letter and word spacing do not clip content. Single-line controls (buttons, chips) never wrap their labels; keep those labels short.
  • Minimum size. 12px is the floor for any text, including chips, credits and photo labels (D12).
  • Contrast is a property of colour, not size: all text meets 4.5:1 regardless of weight, so no text relies on the large-text exception. See colour contrast.
  • Headings are real headings. The role sets the look; the element (h1 to h3) sets the outline. One h1 per page.
  • Uppercase is visual. Write overlines in sentence case in the markup and uppercase them in CSS, so screen readers do not spell them out letter by letter.
