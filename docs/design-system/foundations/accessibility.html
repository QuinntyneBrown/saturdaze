Foundation

Accessibility

Every family member who plans a weekend should be able to: on a small phone, with a screen reader, by keyboard, at 200% text, in bright sun or with motion turned off. Saturdaze commits to WCAG 2.2 level AA, built into the tokens and components so pages get it by default.

WCAG 2.2 AAOwns --target*Owns --focusRingOffset

Overview

Accessibility is decided once, low in the stack, so nobody has to remember it on a Friday afternoon:

  • Tokens carry contrast (every text and edge pair passes in both themes), the focus ring, target sizes and reduced motion.
  • Components carry roles, keyboard behaviour, labelling and states. Each component page has an Accessibility section in the same seven parts (see the contract).
  • Pages carry structure: a skip link, landmarks, one <h1>, headings in order, a unique title.

The checklist below is the commitment. Where the mocks fell short, the fix is recorded in the drift table.

Every page, every time

  • Skip link first, then landmarks; one <h1>; a unique title.
  • Tab through it: every control is reachable, in reading order, with a visible ring.
  • Every control has a name a stranger would understand out of context.
  • Nothing relies on colour, hover or motion alone.
  • It works at 320px and at 200% text.
  • Status changes are announced; errors say how to fix them.

Focus

One shared ring for every interactive element: a 2px outline in --colorStrokeFocus2, drawn --focusRingOffset outside the element so the surface shows between ring and control. It appears on :focus-visible only, so taps and clicks do not trigger it. Text fields replace it with a coral border and a soft halo, which reads as "typing here".

Target sizes

Two sizes, both tokens. The minimum applies to everything; the comfortable size is for the controls a parent hits most on a phone.

24px minimum 44px comfortable Outdoors
The chip's remove glyph is 16px but its hit area is 24px (D27); vote buttons are 28px with 4px between; the ghost row is 44px.
TokenValueWhere
--targetMinEvery target (2.5.8): chips and their remove buttons, text buttons, links in lists and footers, nav summaries, the chip-row height.
--targetComfortableText fields and selects, phone page actions, dialog and form buttons under 720px, menu items, ghost rows, the date tile.
  • Small buttons (32px) only appear in rows, card footers and dialog headers, with at least 4px between neighbours.
  • Inline links inside a sentence are exempt from 2.5.8, but links that stand alone in a list or footer get min-height: var(--targetMin).
  • Grow the hit area with padding or a ::before, not by enlarging the glyph.

Testing

Automated, on every change

  • check_contrast.py over tokens/contrast-pairs.json, both themes. A new pairing gets a new entry first.
  • check_design_system.py over this site: names on every button and link, labels on every field, alt on every image, one <h1>, landmarks, no broken links or fragments, no raw colours.
  • The mocks' .verify.mjs and the e2e responsive audit for overflow at 320 to 1920px.

By hand, for each new screen or component

  1. Keyboard. Unplug the mouse. Tab through everything; open and close every dialog and menu; check the ring is always visible and focus returns where it started.
  2. Screen reader. VoiceOver with Safari on an iPhone (the main way families use Saturdaze), and NVDA with Chrome on Windows. Listen to one dialog, one form with an error and one list of cards.
  3. Zoom and text size. 200% and 400% zoom at 1280px; browser text size at its largest.
  4. Preferences. In Chromium DevTools, emulate prefers-reduced-motion, prefers-contrast: more, forced-colors: active and dark mode.
  5. Touch. A real phone and a tablet: every action reachable without hover; the bottom nav clear of the browser bar.
  6. Text spacing. Apply 1.5 line height, 0.12em letter spacing and 2× paragraph spacing; nothing clips.

The component contract

Every component page's Accessibility section answers the same seven questions, in this order, so a reviewer always knows where to look:

  1. Role and pattern: the native element or ARIA role, with a link to the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices pattern it follows (for example the Dialog (Modal) pattern).
  2. Keyboard: a key table covering Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space, Esc, arrows and Home/End where they apply.
  3. Focus: where focus starts, where it goes on close, how the ring looks on this component.
  4. Labelling: what must be named, how, and what a screen reader announces.
  5. Contrast: the token pairs involved, with live ratios in both themes.
  6. Motion: what animates and what reduced motion does to it.
  7. Touch: target sizes and what replaces hover.
