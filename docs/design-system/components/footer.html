Footer

A single centred line of small links at the end of public and sign-in pages: Terms, Privacy, and a way in or back. It is there when someone looks for it and otherwise stays out of the way.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableAngular: page markup (no component)Landmark <footer>

Overview

  • Site footer (.site-footer) ends the public pages, the ones with the site bar: landing, legal, a shared weekend. It names the product and links Terms, Privacy and Sign in.
  • Auth footer (.auth__foot) sits under the auth card on sign in, create account, reset password and verify email: Terms, Privacy and "Back to Saturdaze".
  • The signed-in app has no footer: the bottom nav occupies the end of the screen on phones. No signed-in mock links to Terms or Privacy yet; the Family page is the natural place. Admin has none either; its sign-in page uses the auth footer.

A footer is not a second navigation. Anything a family needs to do belongs in the page; the footer only holds the legal pages and the way in or out.

Anatomy

  1. Product line"© 2026 Saturdaze": plain text, first in the row.
  2. Links.link--soft: the footer's colour, underlined in --colorNeutralStrokeAccessible; 12px (--typographyCaption1).
  3. RowA centred, wrapping flex row in --colorNeutralForeground3; 12px between items, 6px between lines. The site footer adds 80px above it and 40px below.

Variants

Site footer

Public pages. The <footer> follows <main> so it is the page's contentinfo landmark. On legal, Terms and Privacy point at the fragments of the same page.

Under the auth card. The middle dots are decorative and hidden from screen readers. It is a paragraph inside <main>, not a landmark: these pages are one card and nothing else.

  • Hover darkens the link to --colorNeutralForeground1 and turns the soft underline to full colour.
  • There is no current state. On legal, Terms and Privacy are current in the segments at the top, not in the footer.

Responsive behaviour

  • The row is centred and wraps; at 320px the site footer's four items still fit on one or two lines.
  • The site footer keeps --layoutSpacePageLarge (80px) above it at every width, so it never reads as part of the last section, and --spacingVerticalXXXXL (40px) below so the last link is not at the screen edge or under the iOS home indicator.
  • Under 380px the landing site bar hides "Sign in" (the hero repeats it); other public pages keep it in the bar. The footer's "Sign in" is the constant one; keep it.

Theming

Alias tokens only: --colorNeutralForeground3 for the row, links inheriting it, --colorNeutralStrokeAccessible for the resting underline, --colorNeutralForeground1 on hover. No component tokens. In the dark theme the row is a mid umber-grey on the umber 900 canvas; it stays the quietest text on the page in both themes.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The site footer is a <footer> placed outside <main>, directly in <body>, so it is the page's contentinfo landmark (see APG landmark regions). A <footer> inside <main>, as in the mocks, has no landmark role. The auth footer is a paragraph of links and needs no landmark.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the links in order; the product line is text and is skipped.
EnterFollows the link.

Focus

The shared 2px --colorStrokeFocus2 ring with a 2px offset and a small radius around each link.

Labelling

Link text is the destination: "Terms", "Privacy", "Sign in", "Back to Saturdaze". The middle dots in the auth footer are aria-hidden="true", so a screen reader does not read "dot" between links.

Contrast

Footer text and links on the page (D04)
Hovered link on the page
Link underline on the page
Focus ring on the page

Footer links share the text's colour, so the underline is what marks them as links (1.4.1). Never remove it.

Motion

None.

Touch

Links are 12px text in a 24px-tall box (--targetMin), the 2.5.8 minimum, with 12px between them on a line and 6px between wrapped lines. Do not add links to the row.

Content

  • Site footer: "© 2026 Saturdaze", Terms, Privacy, Sign in. In that order.
  • Auth footer: Terms, Privacy, "Back to Saturdaze" (the landing page).
  • "Terms" and "Privacy", not "Terms of Service" and "Privacy Policy": the page titles are long, the links do not need to be.
  • No social links, newsletter sign-up or sitemap. Saturdaze has five screens.
