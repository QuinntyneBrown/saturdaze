Skeleton

A skeleton draws the outline of what is coming, so the page does not jump when Saturday's blocks or a row of ideas arrive. It is a placeholder for layout, not a message: the words live in a status row beside it.

Overview

Use a skeleton when you know the layout of the content that is loading: the timeline of a day, a grid of idea cards, a list of past weekends. It reserves the space so nothing shifts when real content lands (WCAG's "no surprise" principle and a lower layout shift). Use a spinner when the shape is unknown, and an empty state once you know there is nothing to show.

  • Shapes (.skeleton--text, --text-sm, --title, --disc, --rect, --time) mirror one piece of real content each.
  • Skeleton row (.skeleton-row with .skeleton-row__body) mirrors a timeline block: time, disc, title and subtitle, on the same 56 / 32px columns.
  • Composed card (shapes inside a .card) mirrors an idea card: photo, title, meta.

Show a skeleton only for content that takes longer than about 300ms, and for no more than the layout the screen will really show (six rows per day on the generating page, not twenty).

Anatomy

  1. Time.skeleton--time: 36 × 12px, right-aligned in the 56px time column.
  2. Disc.skeleton--disc: 32px circle on the rail, where the block's icon will be.
  3. Title line.skeleton--text: 12px tall, 60% wide. Every shape is --colorNeutralStencil1 with a shimmer sweep.
  4. Subtitle line.skeleton--text-sm: 10px tall, 40% wide. 8px between lines (.skeleton-row__body).

Variants

Six shapes, named after what they stand in for. Compose them into the layout of the real component; do not invent new shapes per screen.

Title (20px, 45%), text (12px, 60%), small text (10px, 40%), disc (32px) and time (36px), rect (16:9, full width).

Sizes

Shapes take the size of the type or object they replace, so there is no separate size scale. Heights are in rem and follow the user's font size; widths are percentages so lines look ragged like real text.

States

Skeletons have no pointer or focus states: they are not interactive. Their states are loading, loaded, and loading under reduced motion. The real content replaces the skeleton in place, with the same outer size.

Both themes

  • Loading: the stencil sweeps left to right every --durationShimmer (1.4s). The region has aria-busy="true"; a status row says what is happening.
  • Reduced motion: the sweep stops and the shapes stay as flat stencils. The status row's spinner keeps turning, so the page still looks alive (D29).
  • Loaded: swap the whole region at once and remove aria-busy. Do not reveal rows one by one; it reads as flicker and announces piecemeal.
  • Nothing came back: replace the skeleton with an empty state. Failed: replace it with an alert and a retry, never a skeleton that spins forever.

Responsive behaviour

  • Skeleton rows use the block's grid (56px time, 32px disc, flexible body), so they line up with real rows at every width.
  • Skeleton cards sit in .grid-cards: one column on a phone, two from 720px, three from 1024px. Show as many placeholders as fill one screen at that width (three on a phone, six on a desktop), not more.
  • Days stack under 1024px and sit side by side from 1024px (.grid-days); each day shows six rows while generating.
  • Percentage widths keep text lines ragged at 360px and at 1280px.

Theming

The base is --colorNeutralStencil1 (cream 100 in light, a warm umber in dark) and the sweep is --colorNeutralStencil2Alpha, a translucent highlight that is stronger in light (60%) than in dark (6%), so the shimmer is visible without glowing. Both used to be literal rgba() values in the mocks (D17). Shapes keep the radius of what they replace: --borderRadiusSmall for lines, circular for discs, --borderRadiusMedium for photos.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Skeleton shapes are decoration: every shape, or the list that holds them, is aria-hidden="true". The region that is loading carries aria-busy="true" so assistive technology waits for the finished content, and a role="status" row says what is loading. There is no APG pattern; this follows the aria-busy state.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips skeletons entirely. Never put a focusable placeholder (a fake link or button) inside one.

Focus

When the real content replaces the skeleton, focus stays where it was. If the person was focused inside the region (for example on "Regenerate"), keep focus on that control rather than jumping to the new rows.

Labelling

Give the busy region a name that says it is loading when no visible heading does ("Ideas, loading"). Real headings that are already known (Saturday, the forecast) stay visible and readable while the rows load.

Contrast

Placeholder shapes convey no information, so 1.4.11 non-text contrast does not apply to them; they are deliberately faint. The words around them must still pass.

Motion

The shimmer is decoration. Under prefers-reduced-motion it stops (animation: none on .skeleton::after, and --durationShimmer is zeroed). The sweep is slow (1.4s), low contrast and never flashes more than three times a second.

Touch

Not interactive. Do not attach tap handlers to skeletons, and do not let a skeleton cover controls that already work.

Content

  • Skeletons have no text. Put the words in the status row: "Sketching Saturday and Sunday. Usually four to six seconds."
  • Show what you already know for real: day names, dates, the forecast, the page title. Only the unknown parts are placeholders.
  • Match the count to the likely result (six blocks per day), so the page does not grow or shrink a lot when it lands.
