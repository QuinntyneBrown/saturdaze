Card

A card holds one thing the family might do or has done: a place, a restaurant, an event, a past weekend, a submission waiting for review. It shows the facts that decide it and the actions that act on it.

Overview

Use a card when each item in a collection needs its own picture, facts and actions, and people compare items side by side: the Ideas grids, Past weekends, the review queue. Use a list when items are uniform rows a person scans and opens (Who's in, the admin Places list). Use a section to group a page, never a card around a whole section.

  • Default (.card) is a white surface with a hairline border and 16px padding.
  • Media (.card--media with .card__media) leads with a 16:9 photo or a tinted fallback tile. Every idea card and past weekend uses it (28 of the 47 cards in the mocks).
  • Large padding (.card--pad-lg) is for dense, standalone records: review submissions and admin stat cards.
  • Sunk (.card--sunk) is a summary inside another surface, such as the submission recap in a dialog (D11, D23).
  • Locked, dimmed, muted and selected show a decision: this pick is locked in, these picks stepped back, this event is not public yet, this one is chosen.
  • Interactive (.card--interactive) is a card whose only job is to open something. One real link stretches over it (.card__stretch); the card itself is not the control.

Anatomy

  1. Media (optional).media.card__media, 16:9, bleeds to the card edges; credit pill bottom right. A tinted .media--fallback tile when there is no photo.
  2. Head.card__head: optional date tile, then .card__title (--typographyTitle3) and .card__meta in .card__head-text.
  3. Status chip (optional)A .chip as the last child of the head is pushed to the right: Top pick, Locked for dinner, Pending.
  4. Body (optional).card__body, --typographyBody2 in the secondary colour; clamp to two lines in grids.
  5. Chips (optional).card__chips: drive time, ages, tags. Three at most.
  6. Footer (optional).card__footer sits at the bottom (margin-top: auto) so footers line up across a row. Small buttons, primary last.
  7. Container--colorNeutralBackground1, 1px --colorNeutralStroke2, --borderRadiusLarge, 16px padding, 10px gap.

Interactive

When the whole card opens one thing and has no other controls, make the title a link with .card__stretch on a .card--interactive. Its ::after covers the card, so a tap anywhere follows the link, but the accessible control is still just the link.

Sizes

Cards take their width from the grid they sit in (container and grid). The only size choice is padding: 16px by default and 24px with .card--pad-lg (Angular padding="lg"). Locked and selected cards subtract 1px from the padding to absorb their 2px border, so content does not shift when the state changes.

States

Only .card--interactive responds to the pointer. A static card never changes on hover; its buttons do. Focus is drawn on whatever has focus: on a static card that is a footer button, on an interactive card it is the stretched link, and the ring goes round the whole card.

Interactive, hover

  • Hover (interactive only, pointer devices only): border darkens to --colorNeutralStroke1, --shadow16, 2px lift.
  • Focus within: the focused control draws the shared ring. On an interactive card the ring belongs around the card, because the whole card is the hit area: .card--interactive:has(.card__stretch:focus-visible) draws it there and drops the link's own ring, so there is only one.
  • Selected: 2px --colorBrandStroke1 border. The control that chose it carries the state (aria-pressed="true", a checked radio or aria-current); the border only echoes it.
  • Locked: 2px --colorStatusSuccessBackground3 border plus a "Locked" chip.
  • Dimmed (60% opacity) for picks that stepped back; their actions are disabled. Muted (85%) for content only you can see yet. Opacity costs contrast: dimmed meta text falls to about 2.5:1 and muted to about 4.4:1, both under 4.5:1. Until the product fades only the media and chips, say the reason at full contrast outside the faded text (the chip, the section heading, or a body line on a muted card), and never fade a card twice.

Responsive behaviour

  • Grids: .grid-cards is one column on phones, two from 720px and three from 1024px; .grid-cards--2 stops at two (Food's dinner picks). A .card--span card takes the full row (the Top pick on Food).
  • Media stays 16:9 at every width and bleeds to the card's edges; photos never crop faces at the top because object-fit: cover centres them.
  • Footers wrap; on a 360px phone two small buttons fit one row. .card__footer--2up keeps two equal columns at every width. Footers sit at the bottom so a row of cards lines its buttons up.
  • Actions are always visible. Nothing on a card appears only on hover; touch tablets at 720px and wider have no hover (the lesson of D21).
  • Titles wrap; bodies clamp to two lines with .clamp-2; meta lines wrap rather than truncate.

Theming

The card has no component tokens: it reads alias tokens directly, and every variant is a class. In the dark theme the card surface (--colorNeutralBackground1) is a warm umber one step lighter than the page, so cards rise rather than sink; the hairline stays visible because --colorNeutralStroke2 lightens with it. Sunk cards use --colorNeutralBackground3 in both themes. Locked and selected borders switch to their lighter dark-theme steps and stay above 3:1 against the page.

/* A card inside a dialog reads as a recap, not a second dialog */
.dialog__body .card { /* use .card--sunk instead of restyling */ }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

No ARIA pattern: a card is a grouping, not a widget. In a collection it is an <li> in a <ul class="grid-cards">, so screen readers announce "list, 3 items". Standalone records are <article aria-labelledby="…"> pointing at the title (Review submissions). An interactive card is a plain element with one <a> inside; never put role="link" or a click handler on the card, and never wrap a card that contains buttons in an <a>.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the card's controls in reading order: title button, favourite, footer buttons. An interactive card is one tab stop.
EnterFollows the stretched link, or activates the focused button.
SpaceActivates the focused button (favourite, vote, Add to day).

Focus

Controls inside a card show the shared 2px --colorStrokeFocus2 ring. On an interactive card the ring goes round the whole card (2px offset, outside the border), so it matches the area a tap would hit. The card never takes focus itself.

Labelling

The title is a heading at the right level (h3 under a section's h2). Repeated buttons name their object, either in the visible label or with an aria-label that starts with the visible text ("Add to day: Terre Bleu Lavender Farm"), so voice control still works (2.5.3). Photos describe the scene (alt="Rows of lavender running to the horizon"); fallback tiles and photos that repeat the title are aria-hidden or alt="". A date tile is aria-hidden because the meta line says the date in words.

The card's own hairline (--colorNeutralStroke2) is decorative: the card is identified by its content, not its edge, so it does not need 3:1.

Motion

Interactive cards transition border, shadow and a 2px lift over --durationFast. Under prefers-reduced-motion the duration is zero and the lift is instant.

Touch

Footer buttons are 32px tall with 8px between them; an interactive card's whole surface is the target. Favourite and vote toggles keep at least 32px. Never place a small control inside an interactive card: the stretched link would cover it.

Content

  • Title: the place or event's own name, as the venue writes it ("Jack Astor's", "Royal Botanical Gardens"). Past weekends get a short family title ("Bronte Creek + Rec Room").
  • Meta: where and when, joined with a middle dot: "Seafood · Patio on the lake · Port Credit", "Lakeshore Rd · Sat 2pm to 9pm".
  • Body: why it suits this family this weekend, in one or two plain sentences: "Lavender peaks 17 to 24 May, and Mae is old enough to walk the rows this year."
  • Chips: facts that sort the list (drive time, ages, cost), three at most. Status words go in the head chip, not the body.
  • Footer: verb-first, two buttons at most; primary last.
