Spinner

A spinner says "working on it" while Saturdaze plans a weekend, verifies an email or saves a change. It always comes with words, and it keeps turning even when motion is reduced, because it is status, not decoration.

Overview

Use a spinner when something is happening and you cannot show its shape or how far along it is: planning Saturday and Sunday, checking a verification link, retrying after an error. If you know the layout that is coming, use a skeleton. If you know how far along it is (an upload, an ingestion run), use a progress bar. If a single button started the work, put the spinner on that button (aria-busy="true", see button states).

  • Spinner (.spinner) is the bare ring. It never appears alone: pair it with a visible label or visually hidden text inside a role="status" region.
  • Spinner disc (.spinner-disc) rings an icon that names the job: a sparkle while the weekend is drafted, an envelope while an email link is checked.
  • Status row (.status-row) is a card-width row above content that is still loading: "Working through your locks, the forecast and past weekends."
  • Inline status (.inline-status) is a small spinner and a short phrase beside a control: "Saving…", "Checking the forecast…".

Anatomy

  1. TrackFull ring, --colorNeutralStroke1, 3px (--strokeWidthThicker; 2px on --sm).
  2. ArcOne quarter of the ring in --colorBrandStroke1; one turn every --durationSpin (900ms), linear.
  3. LabelWhat is happening, in words. Visible in a status row; visually hidden when the spinner stands alone.
  4. Container (status row)Card surface, 1px --colorNeutralStroke2 border, --borderRadiusLarge, 12 × 16px padding. Carries role="status".

Variants

Pick by where the wait happens: a whole region (status row), a centred card (spinner disc), or next to one control (inline status).

Sizes

Small sits in a line of text or a button; medium sits in a list row or a compact card; large (the default) sits in a status row, an auth card or the middle of an empty region.

States

A spinner has no pointer states. Its states are its lifecycle: it appears when work starts, stays while the work runs, and is replaced by the result or by an error message. Nothing is focusable inside it.

Planning your weekend…
  • Delay: show a spinner only after about 300ms. Work that finishes sooner should not flash one.
  • Long waits: after about 10 seconds, update the label to say why ("Still checking drive times…"), and offer a way out if there is one.
  • Done removes the spinner; the result is the confirmation. Announce it only when the result is not visible where focus is ("Weekend ready").
  • Failed replaces the spinner with an inline message or a banner that says what to do next.

Responsive behaviour

  • Spinner sizes are fixed in rem and do not change between breakpoints; they grow with the user's font size.
  • The status row is full width at every breakpoint. The disc stays 40px and never shrinks (flex: none); the label wraps onto a second line at 360px.
  • On the generating page the status row sits under the page header and above the day columns, so it is the first thing read on a phone and stays in view on a desktop.
  • Inline status wraps with its control. Keep the phrase under about 30 characters so it fits beside a button at 360px.

Theming

The spinner reads two alias tokens: --colorNeutralStroke1 for the track and --colorBrandStroke1 for the arc. The disc icon takes --colorBrandForeground1. In the dark theme the arc lightens to coral 300 and the track to a warm umber, so the arc still stands out at more than 3:1. Re-skin a local spinner by overriding border-color and border-top-color; do not change the turn speed.

/* A spinner on a success well */
.well--accent .spinner { border-top-color: var(--colorStatusSuccessForeground3); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The container is a polite live region: role="status" (implicitly aria-live="polite"). The ring itself is aria-hidden="true"; the words carry the meaning. There is no APG pattern for a spinner; it follows the WAI-ARIA status role. The region that is still loading carries aria-busy="true" (the generating page sets it on <main>) and drops it when the content arrives.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips the spinner: it is not focusable. Controls that cannot act yet are disabled (the generating page disables Share and Add to calendar).
EscNo effect. If the wait can be cancelled, offer a real Cancel button.

Focus

Focus stays where it was. When the work started from a button, the button keeps focus while aria-busy="true"; when the result replaces the region, move focus to its heading only if the person would otherwise be lost (a whole page swapped).

Labelling

The live region must exist in the page before its text changes, or screen readers will not announce it. Render the empty role="status" container first, then insert "Planning your weekend…". Hidden text uses .visually-hidden, never display: none. Do not announce each tick of a long wait; update the text at most every few seconds.

Motion

Under prefers-reduced-motion, transitions and skeleton shimmer stop, but --durationSpin is deliberately kept, so the spinner keeps turning (D29). A frozen ring reads as "stuck", which is worse than motion; one slow, linear turn is not a vestibular trigger. Nothing else on the page should move while a spinner is showing.

Touch

Not interactive, so no target size applies. A spinner never sits on top of a control and never blocks taps on the rest of the page.

Content

  • Say what is happening, as a present participle with an ellipsis: "Planning your weekend…", "Verifying your email", "Saving…".
  • Name what the planner is doing when you can: "Working through your locks, the forecast and past weekends." It explains the wait and builds trust.
  • Give a time only when it is reliable: "Usually four to six seconds."
  • Never "Loading…" on its own, "Please wait" or "Hang tight". Never a percentage on a spinner.
