Toast

A toast says "done" for an action whose result is not on screen: a link copied, a block removed, a day regenerated. When the action can be reversed it offers Undo. It waits above the bottom nav on a phone, bottom right on a wider screen, and then leaves on its own.

Overview

Use a toast for a short confirmation after an action, when the change is not visible where the person is looking or when it can be undone for a few seconds. Suggested uses in Saturdaze:

  • "Swim lessons removed." with Undo, after removing a block from Saturday.
  • "Link copied." after Copy link in the Share dialog.
  • "Saturday regenerated. Your 2 locks stayed put." after Regenerate day.
  • "Photo sent for review." after an admin or family member adds a place photo.

Do not use a toast for anything the person must read or act on. A failed sign-in, a form error or a problem with the whole page is an alert; a field problem is an inline message; a choice is a dialog. A toast that reports an error from background work (a photo upload that failed after the dialog closed) uses the warn tone and stays until dismissed.

See notifications for choosing between toast, alert and inline message.

Anatomy

  1. Tone icon20px, coloured by tone (--sd-toast-icon), decorative: the text carries the meaning.
  2. Text.toast__text, one sentence, 14px body. Wraps; never truncates.
  3. Action (optional).toast__action: one small quiet button, usually Undo.
  4. Close.toast__close: small ghost icon button, aria-label="Dismiss".
  5. ContainerRaised surface, 1px stroke, large radius, --shadow28, at least 52px tall.

Variants

The tone changes the icon colour (and, for warn, the border). The surface stays neutral so a toast never competes with the page.

One size. The toast is as tall as its text needs (52px minimum) and as wide as the region.

The toast itself has no pointer state; its buttons do. Hovering or focusing anywhere in the region pauses the timer.

Stacked and leaving

Up to three toasts stack with an 8px gap, newest at the bottom, nearest the thumb. A fourth removes the oldest. A leaving toast takes data-leaving: it fades and drops 8px over --durationNormal, then is removed from the DOM. Below, the second toast is caught halfway out.

Timing

  • Auto-dismiss after at least 8 seconds; add a second for every ten words over ten.
  • The timer pauses while the pointer is over the region or focus is inside it, and restarts in full when both leave (2.2.1).
  • Warn toasts never auto-dismiss: an error stays until the person dismisses it or retries.
  • Undo is a shortcut, not the only way back: a removed block can still be added again from Ideas.

Responsive behaviour

  • Under 720px the region spans the page margins and sits 24px above the bottom nav (--layoutBottomNavHeight + --spacingVerticalXXL, plus the safe-area inset), so it never covers the nav or the home indicator.
  • From 720px the region is 24rem wide, 24px from the right and bottom edges. There is no bottom nav at this width.
  • Toasts sit above dialogs and menus (--zIndexToast 60 over --zIndexDialog 40 and --zIndexPopover 50), so "Link copied." shows over the open Share dialog; only tooltips sit higher.
  • Text wraps inside the toast; buttons never wrap or shrink.

Theming

The toast reads one component token, --sd-toast-icon; tones set it. The surface is --colorNeutralBackgroundRaised, so in the dark theme toasts are the lightest umber surface and still separate from the page by their stroke and shadow.

/* A new tone for a planner tip */
.toast--brand { --sd-toast-icon: var(--colorBrandForeground1); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Two live regions exist on every page from the first render, both empty: <div class="toast-region" role="status"> for confirmations (polite) and <div class="toast-region" role="alert"> for warn toasts (assertive). Inserting a toast into a region that already exists is what makes it announced; a region created together with its message is often silent. This follows the APG Alert pattern for errors and the status role (4.1.3) for the rest.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabReaches Undo and Dismiss; the region sits right after <main> so it is close in tab order.
Enter / SpaceActivates the focused button.
EscWith focus inside a toast, dismisses it and returns focus to where it was before.

Focus

A toast never takes focus when it appears; it would pull a keyboard user away from their task. When a focused toast leaves (Undo, Dismiss or Esc), focus returns to the element that triggered the action or, if that is gone (a removed block), to the day's title.

Labelling

The text is the message; the icon is aria-hidden. Close is "Dismiss". An action names what it does ("Undo", "Try again"); the toast text gives its object.

Motion

A toast rises 4px and fades in over --durationNormal (sd-rise) and leaves the same way. Under prefers-reduced-motion the duration is zeroed: it appears and disappears without movement.

Touch

Undo and Dismiss are 32px small buttons with 12px between them. On phones the region stays clear of the bottom nav so a tap meant for Ideas never lands on Undo.

Content

  • Past tense, the object first, a full stop: "Swim lessons removed.", "Link copied.", "Photo sent for review."
  • Add one short clause when it reassures: "Saturday regenerated. Your 2 locks stayed put."
  • No "Success!", no exclamation marks, no emoji.
  • Errors say what failed and offer the fix: "Could not upload memorial-park.jpg." + "Try again".
  • Ten words or fewer when you can; a toast is read at a glance.

Sources

No mock uses a toast yet: this is a core component, built so the product has one consistent way to confirm an action. Its geometry follows the mocks' raised surfaces (dialog, menu) and its placement respects the bottom nav and safe area in pages/weekend. The suggested uses come from actions in pages/weekend (remove, regenerate) and pages/dialogs (Share, Copy link, photo upload).

Drift that applies: D29 (reduced motion zeroes durations, not the whole animation system). There is no Angular toast component yet.
