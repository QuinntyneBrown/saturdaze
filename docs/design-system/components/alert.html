Alert

Alerts stay on the page until the situation changes. A banner reports something that happened ("That email and password did not match.") and what to do about it; a well is a quieter note that explains ("Why this: lavender peaks 17 to 24 May").

Overview

  • Banner (.banner): a bordered, tinted message about the state of a form, a page or the account. Info, success, caution or warn. It can carry a title, actions and a close button. .banner--page runs edge to edge under the top bar for a message about the whole app.
  • Well (.well): a borderless tinted note inside content, usually in a dialog: why the planner chose a block, what a lock keeps, what will be lost. Default, accent, primary or warn.

Choose by persistence and urgency. A message that should leave on its own is a toast. A problem with one field is an inline message next to that field. A list of form errors is the error summary in forms. A banner is for everything between: it stays until the person acts or the situation changes.

Which role

UseWhenSaturdaze example
role="alert"An error or warning that appears in response to something the person just did, and needs attention now."That email and password did not match." after Sign in.
role="status"A non-urgent result that appears after an action, or progress that changes while the person waits."Approved · Bronte Creek Maple Magic" after Approve; "Verifying your email".
No roleAnything present when the page or dialog opens. It is read in order with the content around it.Every well ("Why this", "Locked", "The short version"); a page banner already showing on load.

The live region must exist before its text changes: render the empty role="alert" container with the form, then fill it. A banner created together with its text is often not announced.

Anatomy

  1. Icon20px in the tone's foreground, top aligned, decorative.
  2. Title (optional).banner__title: semibold, one line, says what happened.
  3. Text.banner__text: 14px body, what it means and what to do.
  4. Actions (optional).banner__actions: small buttons, the fix first.
  5. Close (optional).banner__close: ghost icon button in the text colour. Only on banners that are safe to dismiss.
  6. ContainerTinted fill, 1px border in the tone, medium radius, 12px × 16px padding.

Responsive behaviour

  • Banners and wells are full width at every size and grow downward; text wraps, nothing truncates.
  • .banner__actions wraps its buttons onto a new line under the text when they do not fit beside each other.
  • .banner--page drops its radius and side borders and spans the viewport under the top bar; on phones it sits above the page header, never over the bottom nav.
  • In a dialog under 720px (a bottom sheet) a well stays inside the scrolling body, so the footer buttons remain in reach.

Theming

A banner reads --sd-banner-bg, --sd-banner-fg and --sd-banner-edge; a well reads --sd-well-bg and --sd-well-icon. Tones map them to the status ramps (info sky, success forest, warning sun, danger terracotta), which have their own dark-theme steps: deep tints with light text.

/* An admin-only "Needs review" banner in the indoor palette */
.banner--review { --sd-banner-bg: var(--colorPaletteIndoorBackground1); --sd-banner-fg: var(--colorPaletteIndoorForeground1); --sd-banner-edge: var(--colorPaletteIndoorBorderActive); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Errors that appear after an action follow the APG Alert pattern (role="alert", assertive). Non-urgent results use role="status" (4.1.3). Content present on load has no role. Do not add aria-live="assertive" to role="alert" as the sign-in mock does; the role already implies it.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches the banner's actions, then Dismiss, in reading order.
Enter / SpaceActivates the focused button. After Dismiss, focus moves to the next focusable element after the banner's old position.

Focus

An alert does not take focus. On sign-in the error is announced and focus stays on the Sign in button; the first invalid field carries aria-invalid="true". When a form has several errors, use the error summary from forms and move focus to it.

Labelling

The text is the message; the icon is decorative. A title is plain text inside the banner, not a heading, unless the banner leads a section. Close is "Dismiss".

Motion

Banners appear and leave without animation; a banner that slides in pushes content and is easy to miss. Content below simply moves.

Touch

Dismiss and banner actions are 32px small buttons with 8px between them.

Content

  • Say what happened in plain words, then what to do: "That email and password did not match." "Rain is now likely on Sunday after 2pm."
  • No blame and no codes. Not "Invalid credentials" or "Error 401".
  • Titles are short statements, no full stop: "Your email is not verified yet".
  • Wells start with their purpose: "Why this", "Locked", "Keeping on Saturday", "5 weekend covers will change".
  • A warn well above a danger button names the loss: "The lavender weekend draft goes away", then what is safe: "Locks and family settings stay."
