Media

Media is the photo frame on idea cards, past weekends and admin place rows. It crops any photo to a fixed ratio, credits the photographer, and falls back to a tinted tile with an icon when a place has no photo yet.

Overview

Use media whenever a photo of a place, event or weekend sits inside another component: the top of a card (.card__media), a place thumbnail in an admin list row, a candidate photo in the review queue. The frame owns the ratio, the crop and the credit, so cards line up in a grid whatever the source photo's shape.

  • 16:9 (.media) is the default for idea and past-weekend cards.
  • 4:3 (.media--4x3) is for thumbnails and side-by-side comparisons (admin place rows, the review queue, photo dialogs).
  • Fallback (.media--fallback with a tone) replaces the photo when there is none, so a card never shows an empty grey box. As a link it becomes the way to add one ("Add a photo" on Past).

Use the cover photo for the full-width hero of a weekend, and an avatar for a person's photo.

The images in docs/mocks/images/*.svg used on this page are illustrated placeholders that stand in for licensed photos. They are not real photographs and must not ship (docs/mocks/README.md). Production sources, licensing and attribution are specified in L2-100.

Anatomy

  1. Frameaspect-ratio 16 / 9 (or 4 / 3), --borderRadiusMedium, overflow: hidden, neutral well behind the photo while it loads.
  2. Photo.media__img: fills the frame with object-fit: cover; always has width, height and alt.
  3. Credit (optional).media__credit: 12px caption on a 72% dark pill at the bottom right.

Variants

Pick the ratio by where the photo lives; pick the fallback tone by what kind of place it is, using the same tones as chips and filters.

Sizes

Media has no size scale. It is as wide as its container and its height follows the ratio, so it scales with the card grid. The only fixed width is the admin place thumbnail (.place-thumb, 72px at 4:3).

From Toronto Zoo
72px place thumbnail (photo and fallback), a 4:3 review photo, a 16:9 card photo. Thumbnails drop the credit; it returns on the place page.

States

A photo frame is usually content, not a control. It has a loading state, a no-photo state, and, only when it is a link tile, a focus state. Focus is forced here with data-state.

Both themes

ActivityFoodEventIndoor
  • Loading: the frame reserves its ratio before the photo arrives (the width/height attributes plus aspect-ratio), so nothing shifts. While data loads, a .skeleton--rect stands in.
  • No photo / failed: swap to the fallback tile with the place's tone. A broken image icon never shows; the product catches the error event and renders the fallback.
  • Focus: only the link tile is focusable; it takes the shared ring outside its rounded corners. On a card the stretched title link carries focus, not the photo.

Responsive behaviour

  • Inside .grid-cards the frame follows the card: one column under 720px, two from 720px, three from 1024px. The ratio holds at every width.
  • On a .card--media the frame bleeds to the card's edges and loses its own radius, so the card's corners clip it.
  • Serve a srcset so phones get a 720px-wide file and desktops a 1200px one; the frame never needs more than 2× its rendered width.
  • Use loading="lazy" below the fold; the first row of cards on Ideas loads eagerly.

Theming

Photos do not change with the theme. Everything around them does: the loading well (--colorNeutralBackground3), the fallback tints and their icons (palette Background1 / Foreground1 pairs), and the credit pill, which stays dark on any photo (--colorBackgroundOverlayStrong with --colorNeutralForegroundStaticInverted, which stay dark in both themes: ink at 72% in light, black at 72% in dark).

/* A frame inside the photo picker drops its own radius and ratio */
.photo-pick__opt .media { border-radius: 0; aspect-ratio: auto; }

Add a new fallback tone by pairing a palette Background1 with its Foreground1; never put a raw tint on the frame.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A native <img> inside a <div>; no ARIA pattern applies. The fallback tile is a <span> (decorative, aria-hidden="true" when the row already names the place) or an <a href> when it adds a photo.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips photos. Stops on a link tile ("Add a photo to Rainy Rec Room").
EnterOn a link tile, opens the photo dialog.

Focus

The link tile uses the shared ring (2px --colorStrokeFocus2, 2px offset). The frame's overflow: hidden does not clip an outline, so the ring stays whole.

Labelling

  • Alt text describes the photo, not the place's name: "Rows of lavender running to the horizon", "A polar bear beside its pool". The card title already names the place.
  • Empty alt (alt="") when the photo adds nothing to the text next to it: admin place thumbnails beside the place name, the "Current primary" comparison photo.
  • The credit is visible text and is read in order after the image; keep it short ("Photo · Jo Doe").
  • Link tiles name the object: aria-label="Add a photo to Rainy Rec Room", since every tile on Past would otherwise read "Add a photo".

The credit pill is measured as if over pure white, the worst case; the 72% overlay keeps the text above 4.5:1 over any photo.

Motion

None. Photos do not fade in; the card lift on hover belongs to the card and stops under prefers-reduced-motion.

Touch

The "Add a photo" tile is the full card width and at least 160px tall at 360px, far above the 24px minimum. Photos themselves are not targets.

Content

  • Alt text: one plain sentence fragment, no "Image of" or "Photo of", no full stop. Describe what a parent would see: "Tulip beds in front of a glass conservatory".
  • Credit: "Photo · Name" for a photographer or source, "From Toronto Zoo" for a venue's own photo, "Your photo" for a family upload. Never a URL in the product (admin dialogs may show the file name).
  • Fallback label: the kind of place in one word ("Activity", "Restaurant", "Event"), or the action on a link tile ("Add a photo").
  • Photos are of the place, never of people who have not agreed to be shown; family uploads stay private to the family.
