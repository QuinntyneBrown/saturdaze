# L1 - High-Level Requirements

> Each L1 names a capability the Saturdaze system shall deliver. Detailed, testable behaviors live in `L2.md`.

## L1-001: Authentication and Account Lifecycle
The system shall let a family register, sign in, sign out, recover a forgotten password, reset it via emailed link, and verify ownership of their email address. Sessions shall persist across page reloads when the user opts in, and shall be revocable. A signed-in user shall be able to personalise their account with a profile photo that replaces their initials wherever the account avatar appears.

## L1-002: Family Profile and Preferences
Each authenticated account shall own a single Family profile capturing home location, members (with ages), recurring commitments (weekly events with day/time), daily rhythm anchors, likes/dislikes, and toggleable preferences (budget, novelty, notifications). The profile shall drive all downstream personalization.

## L1-003: Weekend Itinerary Generation
The system shall generate a Saturday + Sunday itinerary for the family's upcoming weekend, composed of time-blocked activities, meals, drives, downtime, errands, and the family's recurring commitments. A "current weekend" shall always be addressable, and any prior weekend shall be retrievable by ID.

## L1-004: Itinerary Block Control
Users shall be able to lock individual blocks (so regeneration leaves them alone), swap a block for a different suggestion (optionally rejecting prior suggestions), and regenerate the unlocked portion of the weekend. Locked blocks (including commitments) shall never be displaced by automated regeneration.

## L1-005: Activity Discovery and Filtering
The system shall present curated activities filtered by indoor/outdoor, age fit, drive time, and weather suitability, grouped to surface weather-appropriate picks first, rainy-day fallbacks second, and novelty options ("Try something new") third.

## L1-006: Restaurant Discovery and Picks
The system shall surface restaurant picks for a given day and meal slot, filterable by wife-approved status, distance, and patio availability, with a featured top pick that supports voting by each family member and one-tap "lock it in" to the itinerary.

## L1-007: Local Events Discovery
The system shall list local community events within driving distance of home, grouped by Saturday / Sunday / Coming-soon, and filterable by indoor/outdoor and category (Seasonal, Theatre, Festivals, etc.).

## L1-008: Errand Slot Suggestion
Users shall be able to capture a shopping errand with a description, estimated minutes, and optional preferred day. The planner shall place it in an available weekend slot and return the placement for confirmation. Users shall be able to mark an errand done.

## L1-009: Weather Forecast for the Weekend
The system shall display a daily forecast (high, low, condition, note) for Saturday and Sunday of the queried weekend, anchored to the family's home location. Forecast data shall come from a reputable provider, shall be cached to limit upstream load, and shall degrade gracefully when the provider is unavailable.

## L1-010: Saved Weekend History
The system shall persist every completed weekend, let users favourite weekends, rate them 1–5 stars, edit their title, and surface them in a history view with filters (All, Favourites, This year, 5★ only). Low-rated activities shall be flagged as "avoid repeating" so future generations can skip them.

## L1-011: Responsive Web Experience
Every user-facing page shall render usably from 320 px through 1920 px. The application shall use the responsive shell defined in ADR-009: a top bar from 720 px and a bottom navigation below 720 px, with side-by-side weekend days at wider widths and stacked days on mobile.

## L1-012: Security
The system shall protect user credentials and session tokens, enforce authorization on all non-public endpoints, validate every external input, defend against the OWASP Top 10 (injection, broken auth, CSRF, etc.), and never log secrets or personally identifying information in plaintext.

## L1-013: Performance
API endpoints shall respond within budgets appropriate to interactive use, frontend bundles shall respect size budgets, weather and other slow upstream calls shall be cached, and Angular route bundles shall be lazy-loaded so first paint is not blocked by lower-traffic pages.

## L1-014: Accessibility
Every interactive element shall be reachable and operable by keyboard, every meaningful element shall expose an accessible name, color contrast shall meet WCAG 2.1 AA, and dialogs shall trap focus and announce themselves to assistive technology.

## L1-015: Observability and Resilience
The system shall emit structured logs for every request, warn at startup when production-required configuration is missing, log upstream provider failures without crashing, and surface meaningful, non-leaky error messages to clients.

## L1-016: Deployment and Migration Pipeline
Every push to `main` shall run backend and frontend test gates before an Azure resource preflight. When the configured API Web App exists, deployment shall publish the API, apply EF Core migrations to Azure SQL, and publish the Angular frontend in that order. When the Web App is absent, the workflow shall skip that deployment chain and publish a visible explanation.

## L1-017: Administrative CLI
The CLI tool (`saturdaze`) shall support applying migrations, seeding curated data, resetting a development database, and ingesting catalog data on demand. The scheduled worker shall reuse the ingestion application workflow. The CLI shall accept a connection string by flag, environment variable, or configuration.

## L1-018: User-Contributed Local Events
Any authenticated user shall be able to submit a local community event that isn't already in the system (e.g. a Port Credit street festival, a school fair, a one-off concert). Submissions enter a pending state and are not visible to other users until an administrator approves them. The single entry point is the primary "Suggest an event" action in the Ideas · Events header, which opens the submission dialog in place (there is no dedicated screen and no floating affordance — revised 2026-09-02 with the v2 design, see [ADR-009](../adr/ADR-009-v2-responsive-shell.md)). The submission form shall capture title, date, and time as required, default the date and time to one hour from now rounded up to the next hour, and treat end time, location, description, cost, age range, and source URL as optional. A confirmation dialog summarises what was sent and the submission then appears under "Your suggestion" on the events feed until it is reviewed. Approved events appear in the regular [L1-007](#l1-007-local-events-discovery) feed indistinguishable from curated entries; rejected events are removed and the submitter is notified.

## L1-019: Design system: Design Token Foundations
The components library (`frontend/projects/components/src/lib/styles/_tokens.scss`) must define the complete Saturdaze visual vocabulary as `--sd-*` CSS custom properties — color, typography, spacing, radius, elevation, motion, layout, stacking and focus — in a single authoritative stylesheet that every component and every documentation page consumes. No other file may redeclare the token vocabulary (ADR-012).

## L1-020: Design system: Storybook Docsite
The design system must be a Storybook docsite for the Angular `components` library, built as an Angular builder target, branded with the Saturdaze palette and laid out like the Fluent UI v9 docsite: Concepts, Theme, Components and Patterns sections in a fixed sidebar order. It renders the real shipped components with the same global styles the app loads — never a second implementation.

## L1-021: Design system: Component Documentation
Every public `sd-*` component must have a story folder with an autodocs page combining a description, best practices, an inputs/outputs table generated from the component's source and JSDoc, an interactive `Default` story wired to Controls, and one story per meaningful variant or state.

## L1-022: Design system: Concepts Documentation
The docsite must document how to use and extend the library: an introduction with the design principles, a quick start, how components are styled (mock BEM classes, encapsulated SCSS, slot rules), accessibility rules, and the story-authoring convention.

## L1-023: Design system: Theme Documentation
The docsite must document the design tokens on Theme pages that read the token stylesheet itself and paint every specimen with the live custom property — colors, typography, spacing, radii, shadows, motion and layout, including responsive retunes — so the documentation cannot drift from the token source.

## L1-024: Design system: Pattern Catalog
The docsite must render whole-screen compositions of real components for the product's key flows — shell navigation, weekend plan, ideas browsing, authentication, family preferences and dialogs — with viewport controls matching the e2e projects so real media queries fire.

## L1-025: Design system: Accessibility Checks
Every story must be checked for accessibility violations in the docsite, and the docsite itself must be keyboard-operable through Storybook's built-in navigation and search.

## L1-026: Design system: Quality Gates and Deployment
A Storybook build must gate every pull request, and an automated, path-filtered pipeline must deploy the static docsite to its own Azure Static Web App when the components library changes.

## L1-027 → L1-030: Retired
The standalone catalog's manifest, custom search, standalone-folder integrity and catalog-shell accessibility requirements were retired with `design-system/` (ADR-012); their capabilities are provided by Storybook built-ins under L1-020 → L1-026.

## L1-031: Catalog Ingestion
The system shall discover local events, activities, and restaurants through provider-backed web search and upsert validated records into the shared catalogs. On-demand and scheduled triggers shall share one ingestion workflow, with run audit records, configurable provider settings, dry-run support, and bounded run frequency.

## L1-032: Place Location and Imagery
Every catalog place (activity, restaurant, local event, and approved event submission) shall carry a geographic location and may carry one or more licensed photos with visible attribution. Location and imagery shall be captured by ingestion, curation, and user submission, shall be stored with their provenance, and shall be served to clients in a form that is fast, safe, and degrades to a non-photo presentation when no image is available. (Added 2026-10-06 from the Wanderlog layout study; mocks: `docs/mocks/pages/weekend.html`, `ideas.html`, `ideas.food.html`, `ideas.events.html`, `past.html`. These replaced the earlier two-day Weekend, icon-led Ideas, and text-only Past mocks the same day.)

## L1-033: Photo-Led Discovery
The Ideas screens shall lead each place card with its photo so a family can recognise a place at a glance, and shall let the family add any idea directly to Saturday or Sunday. The planner shall fit the added idea around locked blocks and commitments and show where it will land before the family confirms.

## L1-034: Weekend Cover Photo and Photo Memories
Each weekend shall have a cover photo, defaulting to the photo of its day highlight, that the family can change to another stop's photo or to a photo they upload. The cover shall lead the Weekend screen, identify each weekend in Past, and appear as the preview image of a shared weekend link. Family-uploaded photos shall be private to the family and to holders of that weekend's share link.

## L1-035: Itinerary Map and Travel Legs
The Weekend screen shall show the travel between consecutive stops (time, distance, and a directions link) inline in the timeline, and shall offer a per-day map with numbered pins matching numbered stops in the timeline. On wide screens the map shall sit beside the day's itinerary; on narrow screens it shall sit above it at a compact size. The two views shall stay in sync and the map shall never be the only way to get route information.

## L1-036: Catalog Photo Administration
Administrators shall maintain the catalog's place photos from Saturdaze Admin, a second web application in the workspace that shares the family app's component and data libraries, design tokens, sign-in and API but ships and deploys on its own. An administrator shall be able to measure photo coverage and health per catalog, find places with missing or poor imagery, add curated photos by upload or by URL, edit alt text, attribution and licence, choose the primary photo, remove photos, and review the photos that ingestion brought in. Every photo shown to families shall be curated or administrator-reviewed, seeding and ingestion shall never undo an administrator's decision, and every change shall be auditable. Family-uploaded photos stay private to the family and outside the admin application. (Added 2026-10-06 from `docs/prd/admin-photo-management.md`.)
